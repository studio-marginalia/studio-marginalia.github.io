Beta Reading

Beta reading is a read-through of your finished manuscript by an outside perspective. This is done before publishing, querying or hiring an editor.

Its value lies in understanding the experience of reading your story, from the eyes of your potential audience.

Good beta reading gives you a map of your work by providing honest feedback on story elements such as plot, pacing and character development; as well as breaking down its strengths and weaknesses, and where potential readers would get confused.

The Reader Report

The report pulls together my overall experience of the read. It covers:

01
First impression

What I thought in the first 10 pages, and what hook (if any) pulled me in.

02
What worked

Specific scenes, characters, or turns that just work.

03
What didn't work

Where the story lost me. Pacing slumps, character decisions that felt off, and moments where I drifted or disengaged.

04
Character notes

Who I connected with, who felt flat, and where motivations became (or failed to become) clear.

05
Pacing

A rough sense of the book's rhythm. Where it rushed, dragged, or found its groove.

06
Confusion points

Specific moments where I had to re-read, guess, or put the book down to figure out what was happening, plus any contradictions or continuity slips I caught.

07
Audience fit

Who I think this book is for, based on what I read.

08
Final impression

The last thing I thought when I closed the book.

Frequently Asked Questions

What's the difference between a manuscript analysis and a beta read? +

A beta read isn't as thorough or as comprehensive as a manuscript analysis, but it's an economical way to find out, in broad strokes, where your story works and where it doesn't.

Will a beta read replace a manuscript analysis? +

It depends. If you've already had an edit and want a beta read of your revised draft, it can. It can also work if you have a large team of beta readers and a system for combining their feedback into a coherent revision plan. Without knowing your specific circumstances, I can't promise it will.

Will working with you get me published? +

No reader can promise that, and you should be skeptical of anyone who does.

A beta read is a strong step towards strengthening your manuscript and giving it a better chance at whatever comes next.

Will you edit or rewrite my manuscript? +

No. I don't line edit, copyedit, or rewrite. I simply tell you what I see. I also don't suggest ways to improve your manuscript.

If a sentence-level issue is affecting clarity or the reading experience, I'll flag it. But flagging isn't fixing. To reiterate: rewriting, line/copy editing, and offering suggestions aren't part of the service.

Do you offer sample reads for this service? +

I only offer sample reads for Manuscript Analysis. There are a number of reasons for this, but the one that encapsulates them all is that it is simply not necessary for a beta read.

How long does the read take? +

Within 15 business days.

If you have a specific deadline, let me know. I'll confirm whether I can accommodate it before you book.

Do you use AI? +

No. Your manuscript is read and responded to by me, a real person, start to finish. I don't use AI to read, summarize, or generate any part of the feedback.

Is my manuscript kept confidential? +

Yes. Your work is never shared, and is deleted from my personal devices after the follow-up period is over. I always work on contract and I'm happy to sign your own contract or NDA agreement on request.

What format do I send my manuscript in? +

Word (.docx) files or shared Google Doc, since both let me leave in-line comments right in the manuscript.
