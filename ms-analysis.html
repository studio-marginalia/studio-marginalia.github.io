Manuscript Analysis

A manuscript analysis is a big-picture edit of your story, done through close reading and honest, thorough diagnosis rather than a line-by-line rewrite.

I read your manuscript at least twice; as a reader in the first read, then stepping back as an editor in the second to consider the manuscript as a whole. I then write you a detailed editorial letter covering everything that shapes your book.

This is a complete alternative to developmental editing. Strong, specific feedback plus a clear execution plan is all you need to get your book to its best draft yet.

A Minimum of Two Full Reads

Your manuscript gets at least two complete read-throughs.

The first read is experiential

To analyze your book, I have to experience it first. So the first read has one purpose: to let myself be swept away in your world.

The second read is analytical

Now I go back with an editor's eye and, scene by scene, I ask why. Why did I see that twist from a mile away? What could hide it from the reader? Why do these characters feel like the same person? Maybe they should be one. Why am I learning in chapter 5 information best suited for an early scene in chapter 2? Why, why, why.

Some manuscripts are more complex or harder to untangle, such as those with dual timelines or large casts, and they may need a third read. If yours does, it's included at no extra cost.

Marginal Notes

Your manuscript comes back annotated, so every point in the letter can be found on the page. When the letter says the middle sags, the margins show you the exact scenes where it happened.

The Editorial Letter

This is the heart of the service and the document you'll return to again and again while revising. It's a written analysis of your entire manuscript, and its length depends entirely on what your book needs.

A manuscript with fewer issues gets a shorter letter, and one with more to untangle gets a longer one. A shorter letter isn't less care. It means your draft is closer to where it needs to be.

Either way, you get the same depth of attention and thoroughness in every point I raise.

What I Look At

Here's some of what the letter covers:

01
Plot:

Cause and effect, logic, stakes, and whether the payoff earns the setup.

02
Pacing:

Where the book moves, drags, or rushes, and how the rhythm affects the reading experience.

03
Character:

Motivation, arcs, consistency, and relationships.

04
POV & Narrative Distance:

Whose head we're in, how deep we go, and where the distance slips.

05
Tone & Voice:

The feeling the writing creates and whether it stays consistent from start to finish.

06
Openings, Endings, & Turning Points:

Whether the first pages hook, the ending lands, and the key shifts in between carry their weight.

07
Setting & Worldbuilding:

Whether the where and when feel real and stay continuous, with no slips in the logic or the calendar.

08
Dialogue & Scene Work:

Whether conversations sound natural and reveal something, and whether each scene earns its place.

09
Clarity & Gaps:

Moments where information is missing, confusing, or revealed too early or too late.

10
And More:

Anything else your manuscript calls for. The letter follows your book rather than a template, so it covers whatever your story needs.

How the Feedback Works

Each area of your manuscript is explored from two perspectives: what's working and where there's room to improve.

I am specific about what's working, so you know exactly what to protect as you revise.

Where there's room for improvement, I go beyond simply pointing it out. You'll know:

1
The Why

Every problem comes with its cause. When you know why a scene drags or a character falls flat, you can fix it properly, and you're far less likely to make the same mistake in your next book.

2
The Way Forward

You leave knowing what to change in your next draft, and where to start.

Know Your Editor

I'm Hope, the editor behind Studio Marginalia.

Before this, I was a ghostwriter for genre fiction. I started on Fiverr, and my 8th client pulled me off the platform. We ended up writing 43 books together over four and a half years.

Eventually, I burned out on the 3,000-word daily deadlines. So I stepped away from writing entirely. I decided that if I ever came back, it would be for my own stories, not someone else's.

But even after I left ghostwriting, my former client kept coming back for help with her manuscripts. We'd always worked well together, so I never minded. She also sent her friends my way: other writers who wanted a second eye on their books.

The work was a welcome change from drafting on deadline. That's when I realized something unexpected. I loved figuring out how to make stories work as much as I loved telling them.

Finding what's not working, understanding why, and knowing what to do about it turned out to be where I'm sharpest.

This service is what that skill became.

Frequently Asked Questions

What's the difference between a manuscript analysis and a developmental edit? +

They cover the same ground: the big-picture elements of a story. The difference is in who I am and how I work. I'm not a credentialed developmental editor, and I don't market myself as one.

I'm a ghostwriter who spent years writing and diagnosing my client's books, so analyzing what makes a story work (and why it doesn't) is the skill I've sharpened most.

My process is built around reading closely, reacting honestly, and explaining clearly. You get a detailed editorial letter, a record of my reader experience in the margins, and support while you revise. For many authors, that's all they need.

Is a manuscript analysis a replacement for a developmental edit? +

For many authors, yes. It's an alternative path, not a first step you must follow with something else.

Will working with you get me published? +

No editor can promise that, and you should be skeptical of anyone who does.

A manuscript analysis can strengthen your manuscript and give it a better chance at whatever comes next. The decisions you make and the outcomes they create depend on you.

Will you line/copy edit or rewrite my manuscript? +

No. I don't line edit, copyedit, or rewrite, and the writing stays entirely yours. What I do is find the problems, explain why they're problems, and offer several options for how to fix them, so you can choose the solution that suits your story.

If a sentence-level issue is hurting clarity or the reading experience, I'll flag it, but the fix is yours to make.

Why do you read the manuscript at least twice? +

The first read captures the authentic reader experience, with no stopping to analyze. The second is analytical, and it's where I figure out why the first read went the way it did. Some manuscripts, such as those with dual timelines or big casts, need a third read. If yours does, it's included at no extra cost.

How long is the editorial letter? +

It depends entirely on your manuscript. A book with fewer issues gets a shorter letter, and a book with more to untangle gets a longer one. A shorter letter isn't less care. It means your draft is closer to where it needs to be.

How long does the edit take? +

Within 35 business days.

If you have a specific deadline, let me know. I'll confirm whether I can accommodate it before you book.

Do you use AI? +

No. Your manuscript is read, analyzed, and responded to by me, a real person, start to finish. I don't use AI to read, summarize, or generate any part of the feedback.

Can I try your editing before I book? +

Yes. Send me the first 2,000 words of your manuscript for a free Sample Edit.

You'll get a chance to see how I read your work and the kind of feedback I provide before deciding whether we're a fit.

Do I need to know what's wrong with my manuscript first? +

No. You might have a specific concern, or you might simply know the manuscript isn't quite working and can't see why.

You don't need to diagnose your own manuscript before coming to me. That's what the manuscript analysis is for.

Is my manuscript kept confidential? +

Yes. Your work is never shared, and deleted from my personal devices after the revision period is over. I always work on contract and I'm happy to sign your own contract or NDA agreement on request.

What format do I send my manuscript in? +

Word (.docx) files or shared Google Doc, since both let me leave marginal notes right in the manuscript.
